Energy certificate

{
  "answer": "…",
  "E_final": 0,
  "threshold": 0.5,
  "margin": 0.5,
  "sweeps": 0,
  "valid": null,
  "model": "handset-1w"
}

The certificate is the product: {answer, E_final, threshold, margin, sweeps}. The threshold is fitted from solution energies, so a positive margin means "at least as good as a training solution". Cheap, tamper-evident, deny-by-default compatible.

Why this is interesting

The energy is the classical Hopfield–Tank mean-field free energy with pairwise couplings: same digit in a peer pair costs 1. That single rule is the model. No GPU, no backprop-through-time, no sampling in training — in fact, no training at all for this demo.

The paper's provocative claim — and our audit confirms it — is that benchmarked "reasoning" by modern looped Transformers on Sudoku-class tasks is just constraint propagation, re-learned slowly. A 1980s dynamical system captures it with 3–4 orders of magnitude less compute.

Our honest reproduction on equally extreme boards (24–27 clues, unique solutions): the paper's learned model claims 99.996%; our faithful pure-numpy fit lands 0.67% — and the failure trail is the real contribution: solutions are strong attractors (97.5% of perturbed solutions relax back), but positives-only pseudolikelihood training rewards off-diagonal decoy entries (min off-diagonal cost −0.5506), producing spurious deep minima at solution depth.
